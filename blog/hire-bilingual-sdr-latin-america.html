How to hire a bilingual SDR from Latin America

By the BL Agency team8 min readUpdated November 2026

Short answer. A good bilingual SDR from Latin America works your US hours, costs a fraction of a US hire, and can be on calls within two weeks. The hard part is not finding candidates. It is testing whether they can actually sell in English when a buyer pushes back.

Why US companies hire sales reps in Latin America

Three things make the region work for outbound: time zone, language and cost.

  • Time zone. Latin America sits inside the US business day. A rep in Lima, Bogotá or Mexico City can cover Eastern or Pacific hours without working nights, which is where offshore teams usually burn out.
  • Language. There is a deep pool of people who have sold to US buyers in English for years, and who can also run Spanish speaking accounts when you need them.
  • Cost. Salaries are lower because cost of living is lower, not because the work is worse.

What a bilingual SDR from Latin America costs

Numbers vary by country, seniority and model, so treat these as ranges, not quotes:

  • One staffing firm, Hire With Near, puts a mid-level SDR or BDR at about $2,000 to $2,500 a month, and a mid-level AE at $2,500 to $3,000.
  • Somewhere estimates a US junior SDR at about $55,000 a year fully loaded, against roughly $21,600 a year for an experienced junior SDR in Latin America.
  • If you want a managed outbound team rather than one hire, market pricing for outsourced SDR services runs from about $3,000 to $15,000 a month.

Two costs are easy to miss. The first is commission: most good reps expect a variable on top of base. The second is a bad hire. A rep who looks great on paper and cannot hold a call costs you two or three months of pipeline, not just salary.

SDR, cold caller or appointment setter?

These titles get mixed up, and the mix up leads to the wrong hire.

  • SDR or BDR: researches accounts, runs outreach across email, LinkedIn and phone, qualifies, and hands meetings to a closer. Best for B2B with a defined ideal customer.
  • Cold caller: phone first, high volume. Needs very strong spoken English and a thick skin. Best when you have verified direct dials.
  • Appointment setter: books calls for a closer, often from inbound or warm lists. Common in coaching and high ticket services.
  • Account Executive: runs discovery to close. Hire this only if you already have pipeline to give them.

How to test a candidate in 20 minutes

This is the screen we run at BL Agency before anyone reaches a client. It fits in twenty minutes.

  1. Switch to English without warning. Start in Spanish if you like, then move to English around minute eight. You learn more from how they handle the switch than from any certificate.
  2. Ask for their last closed deal, flat. "Name the company and the year." A candidate who answers with a methodology instead of a name is either out of practice or avoiding the question. Ask once more, plainly.
  3. Ask for their own numbers. Calls per day, meetings per month, quota and attainment. Real reps know them without thinking.
  4. Run a two minute cold call roleplay. Play a busy US buyer and push back twice. You are listening for whether they stay calm, ask a question and keep the call alive.
  5. Confirm the basics. Which US hours they can cover, a quiet place to take calls and stable internet.

Red flags we see often

  • "Advanced English" on the CV and no English in the interview.
  • No numbers at all, only adjectives.
  • Only B2C or inbound experience for an outbound B2B seat.
  • A comp expectation far from the seat. Ask about it early, not at the offer.
  • A CV that looks written for an AI screener. We have seen hidden instructions to AI tools pasted into CVs. Read it yourself.

Engagement models

  • Contractor through a partner: the partner invoices you monthly and pays the rep. Fast to start and easy to replace if it does not work.
  • Direct hire: you hire the rep yourself and pay the partner a one time placement fee.
  • Recruiting retainer: a monthly fee for an embedded recruiter when you are hiring several reps.
  • Managed outbound: you do not hire a rep at all. A team runs outbound for you. At BL Agency that is the Agent Base: AI agents plus a dedicated Agent Base manager who reviews every email.

Questions people ask first

How much does a bilingual SDR from Latin America cost?

Most US companies budget roughly $2,000 to $3,000 a month for a mid-level SDR or BDR hired through a partner, depending on country, seniority and the engagement model. Commission is usually on top.

Do Latin American SDRs work US hours?

Yes. Latin America sits inside the US business day, so a rep can cover Eastern, Central, Mountain or Pacific hours without working nights.

How do I test a candidate's English?

Do part of the interview in English without warning and run a short cold call roleplay where you push back. A CV that says advanced English tells you very little.

Can I hire the rep directly instead of through an agency?

Yes. Many partners offer direct hire for a one time fee, or a contractor model where they handle invoicing and payment and you can convert the rep later.
