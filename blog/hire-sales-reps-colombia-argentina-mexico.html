Hiring sales reps in Colombia, Argentina or Mexico: how to choose

By the BL Agency team13 min readNovember 9, 2026

Key takeaways

  • All three countries have strong sales talent. Choose by your buyers' hours, the language they buy in and the role, not by a country ranking.
  • Colombia led US company placements in Latin America in 2025 and works on US Eastern time; Argentina leads the region on English; Mexico matches US Central time.
  • National English averages do not predict the person you hire. Test every candidate on a live call in English.
  • The screen matters more than the country: a structured interview and a cold call roleplay tell you more than a passport.

US companies that decide to hire sales reps in Latin America usually ask the next question within a day: which country? Colombia, Argentina and Mexico come up first, and the advice online often reads like a ranking, with one winner for every company.

There is no single winner. This guide compares the three countries on what changes the outcome for a sales hire: where US companies actually hire, English, time zones, the markets your buyers are in and the kind of role you are filling. It ends with how to screen a rep in any of the three, because a good process matters more than the flag on the CV.

01The short answer: choose by your buyers, not by a ranking

Choose Colombia when you need SDRs and cold callers on US Eastern hours from the deepest pool US companies are hiring from today; Argentina when English depth and complex or technical sales matter most; and Mexico when your buyers are on Central or Mountain time or speak Spanish. In all three, the individual candidate matters more than the country.

Three questions settle most decisions:

  • When do your buyers pick up the phone? The rep's working day should cover your buyers' day without anyone working at night.
  • What language do your buyers buy in? English only, or English and Spanish? If Spanish, which market?
  • What is the role? High volume cold calling, written outbound, or closing complex deals as an AE.

Once those are clear, the country often chooses itself. If they are not clear, start there before you post a job. A company that hires an excellent rep in the wrong time zone, or a Spanish speaker for an English only market, has a fit problem that no amount of coaching fixes.

One thing this guide leaves out on purpose is pay. Compensation depends on the role, the seniority and how you hire, and country averages are a poor guide to what a strong sales candidate expects. Ask your hiring partner or employer of record for current figures for your exact role.

02Where US companies are hiring in Latin America

Colombia and Argentina are where US companies hired most in recent data, with Mexico further down but growing. Hiring firm data is not a census, but it shows where companies like yours are finding people today.

According to Near's 2026 State of LatAm Hiring Report, based on more than 2,000 placements by US companies, Colombia became the top hiring destination in 2025, moving up from third place two years earlier. The same report found that BDR and SDR roles led Latin American hiring for the second year running, and expected sales roles to stay the largest category in 2026.

The picture moves. In Near's first half of 2026 data, as reported by TechHR Series, Brazil took first place for the first time and Mexico rose to 12% of placements, as hiring spread across more countries. Brazil is a strong market too, but its first language is Portuguese, which matters for any role that also sells in Spanish.

How to read this:

  • Popular means proven. A country where many US companies already hire sales reps has people used to US buyers, US tools and US working hours.
  • Popular also means competition. Strong candidates in the busiest markets get several offers. Move fast and run a clear process.
  • Share is not quality. The data says where hiring happened, not where the best reps are.

03English: what country averages tell you, and what they do not

Argentina has the highest average English proficiency in Latin America, while Colombia and Mexico score lower on average, but every one of the three has many professionals who sell in English every day. The national average tells you how deep the pool is, not whether the candidate in front of you can run a cold call.

In the EF English Proficiency Index 2025, which covers 123 countries and territories, Argentina ranks 26th in the world and sits in the high proficiency band. Colombia is in the low band and Mexico in the very low band. Those scores come from adults who chose to take EF's online test, so they describe a broad population, not sales professionals.

What that means in practice:

  • In Argentina, you will usually see more candidates with strong written and spoken English, which helps for email heavy outbound and for AE roles with long conversations.
  • In Colombia, the pool is large and many sellers have years of experience with US buyers, often from contact centers and US sales teams. Screening is what separates them.
  • In Mexico, bilingual sellers cluster in business hubs and in companies that already sell to the US. The national average hides a strong top end.

For a sales hire, test English the way the job uses it: on the phone, under pressure, with an objection. A written test or a certificate does not show how someone handles "I am busy, what is this about?" in the first ten seconds. 04Time zones and working hours

All three countries share most of the US working day, but they line up with different US time zones: Colombia with Eastern, Mexico with Central and Argentina one to two hours ahead of Eastern. For cold calling, the best match is the one where the rep's normal day covers your buyers' best calling hours.

The details:

  • Colombia stays on UTC-5 all year. It matches US Eastern in winter and runs one hour behind it when the US moves to daylight saving time.
  • Mexico ended daylight saving time for most of the country in 2022, as reported by timeanddate. Mexico City stays on UTC-6 all year, which matches US Central in winter. Some border areas still follow US clock changes.
  • Argentina stays on UTC-3, so a rep in Buenos Aires starts the day one or two hours ahead of New York. That suits East Coast teams; it means a long day for reps calling the West Coast.

Plan the hours with the rep before you hire, not after. Write down the calling block, the overlap with your team for coaching and how the hours change when the US clocks move. A rep who knows the schedule from the first interview is far less likely to burn out on late calls.

For teams that sell to all US time zones, a mix works well: a rep in Argentina or Colombia covering the East Coast morning and a rep in Mexico covering the Central and Mountain afternoon.

05Market fit: Spanish speaking buyers and the role you are filling

If your buyers speak Spanish, match the rep to the market: Mexico is often the natural fit for buyers of Mexican origin in the US, while Colombia and Argentina are strong for Latin American buyers in their regions and for English only roles. If your buyers only speak English, choose on English, hours and the role.

According to Pew Research Center, people of Mexican origin made up about 60% of the US Hispanic population in 2021. For a company selling to Spanish speaking business owners in Texas, California or the Southwest, a rep from Mexico may share vocabulary, references and a sense of how those buyers talk about their business. That can shorten the time to trust on a call.

The role matters as much as the market:

  • High volume cold calling. Look for resilience, a clear voice on the phone and experience with US buyers. Colombia has a deep pool of reps with exactly that background.
  • Written outbound and research. Strong written English and careful account research matter most. Argentina's English depth helps here, but strong writers exist in all three.
  • AEs and complex sales. Look for discovery skills, product understanding and comfort with long conversations. Senior AEs are scarcer everywhere, so widen the search to all three countries.
  • Bilingual SDRs. Test both languages on a call. Fluency in Spanish does not guarantee a professional tone in it.

06How you hire: contractor, employer of record or a partner

US companies usually hire sales reps in these countries in one of three ways: as an independent contractor, through an employer of record that employs the rep locally, or through a hiring partner that finds and vets candidates. The right choice depends on how long the role will last, how much control you need and how much risk you are willing to carry.

A short comparison:

  • Independent contractor. Fast to set up and flexible. Local rules on who counts as an employee differ by country, so a full time rep who works only for you may need a different setup. Get local advice.
  • Employer of record. A local company employs the rep on your behalf and handles payroll, benefits and labor law. Slower to set up, but clearer for long term, full time roles.
  • Hiring partner. Finds, screens and presents candidates, so your team only interviews people who already passed a sales screen. It can work with either of the setups above.

Country differences show up here more than anywhere else. Labor rules, notice periods, holidays and payment logistics all vary between Colombia, Argentina and Mexico, and they change. This is not legal advice: ask a local employment lawyer or a reputable employer of record before you sign anything long term.

Whatever the setup, budget time for ramp. The Bridge Group puts average SDR ramp at about three months, and a remote rep needs the same structured start as a local one. Our SDR onboarding plan lays out the first 90 days.

07How to screen a sales rep in any of the three countries

Screen every candidate the same way, whatever their country: a short structured interview in English, a live cold call roleplay with a real objection and a reference from someone who managed them in sales. This tells you more than the country, the CV or a language certificate.

A screen that works:

  • Start in English, without warning. The first minutes show how the candidate sounds when a buyer picks up.
  • Ask about real numbers they owned. Meetings booked, calls per day, the segment they sold to. Vague answers are a signal.
  • Run a live roleplay. Give a short brief on a buyer and play the prospect, with a busy opener and one objection. Listen for the reason, the question and the recovery.
  • Check hours and setup. Quiet room, reliable internet and a clear yes to the calling hours you need.
  • Talk to a sales manager reference. Ask what the rep was best at and what they needed help with.

08Bringing it together

Colombia, Argentina and Mexico all have strong sales talent. The right choice depends on your buyers' hours, the language they buy in and the role, and the screen you run matters more than the country.

  1. Start with your buyers: their time zone, their language and the role you need.
  2. Colombia fits SDRs and cold callers on US Eastern hours, from the market US companies hired from most in 2025.
  3. Argentina fits roles where English depth matters most, such as AEs and email heavy outbound.
  4. Mexico fits Central and Mountain time coverage and Spanish speaking US markets.
  5. Choose a setup (contractor, employer of record or partner) with local advice.
  6. Screen the same way everywhere: English on a live call, a roleplay and a sales reference.

09Frequently asked questions

Which Latin American country is best for hiring sales reps?

There is no single best country. Colombia led US company placements in 2025 and works on US Eastern time, Argentina has the highest English proficiency in the region and Mexico matches US Central time. Choose by your buyers and the role.

Is English better in Argentina, Colombia or Mexico?

On national averages, Argentina scores highest in the EF English Proficiency Index 2025, followed by Colombia and then Mexico. All three have many professionals who sell in English, so test each candidate on a live call.

Which country has the best time zone overlap with the US?

Colombia matches US Eastern time in winter and is one hour behind in summer. Mexico City matches US Central in winter. Argentina is one to two hours ahead of US Eastern.

Should I hire a rep from Mexico to sell to Spanish speaking US customers?

Often, yes. Most US Hispanics are of Mexican origin, so a rep from Mexico may share vocabulary and references with those buyers. Still test both English and Spanish on a call.

Can I hire a sales rep in Latin America as a contractor?

Many US companies do, but local rules on who counts as an employee differ by country. For a long term, full time role, an employer of record is often clearer. Get local legal advice.

How long does it take to hire a sales rep in Latin America?

With a hiring partner, you can meet interviewed candidates within days. At BL Agency, we send three interviewed profiles in 72 hours. Plan about three months for the rep to ramp.

How do I test a sales candidate from Latin America?

Run a short structured interview in English, a live cold call roleplay with a real objection and a reference check with a former sales manager.
