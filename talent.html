Our approach

A recruiter can't tell you whether someone can sell.

Most staffing firms match keywords. Someone reads a CV, sees "SDR" and "outbound," and forwards it. Nobody on their side has ever written a sequence, handled an objection, or missed a quota — so nobody on their side can tell the difference between a rep who sounds good and a rep who sells.

We're a sales agency that also places people. Every candidate we send you has been interviewed in English by a working account executive, put through a live cold-call roleplay, and pushed on until they either adapt or don't. What we're watching for is what happens when a prospect says no — because that's the job.

The result is a shorter list. Three profiles, not thirty. If we don't have the right person for your role, we say so instead of sending you someone to look busy.

FAQ

What US companies ask before hiring nearshore.

How do we engage them — contractor or employee?

Whichever fits your company. Most engagements run as contractors invoicing through us, so you get a single monthly invoice and no payroll or employer-of-record overhead. If you'd rather bring someone on directly, we structure it that way instead. We agree the model before you interview anyone.

How good is their English, really?

Every candidate is interviewed in English by a working account executive and put through a live cold-call roleplay before they enter our pool. If they can't hold a conversation under pressure with a prospect pushing back, they don't get sent to you. You'll also interview them yourself before deciding.

What if the rep doesn't work out?

We replace them at no additional cost within the first 30 days. After that, the engagement runs month to month with 30 days' notice on either side.

Can we hire them directly later?

Yes — we'll talk about it. There's a conversion fee, agreed in the contract, that converts the placement into a direct hire. What we ask is that it goes through us rather than around us.

Do they work our hours or theirs?

Yours. Peru, Colombia and Ecuador are on Eastern Time year-round, so covering a US business day is a normal nine-to-six for them. Pacific Time coverage is possible and priced separately, because it's a genuinely harder shift.

How is this different from an offshore staffing firm?

Two things: we only place sales roles, and the screening is done by people who sell for a living. A generalist staffing firm can place a developer, a designer and an SDR from the same process. We can't — and that's the point.
